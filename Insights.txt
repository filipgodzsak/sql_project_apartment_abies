SQL Reservation Analytics — Key Insights

Dataset contains 327 reservation records across multiple years; overall data quality is good with only a few anomalies detected (one zero-night stay, two invalid date ranges, one price mismatch, several null commissions normalized to zero).

Booking channels were inconsistent in raw data (e.g., Booking, booking, book, air, airbnb). Channels were normalized using CASE + pattern matching into three analytical groups: booking, airbnb, priamo. This enabled correct aggregation and comparison.

Total revenue is driven primarily by the booking channel (~53% revenue share). Direct bookings contribute ~31% and airbnb ~16%.

Booking has the highest ADR (~91) and highest total and net revenue despite the highest commission rate (~14%). Direct channel has near-zero commission and the strongest margin efficiency but lower ADR.

Average length of stay is slightly above 3 nights and ADR is ~82 overall, indicating stable short-stay booking behavior.

Strong seasonality is present. July is consistently the top revenue month, with the main high season in summer and a smaller secondary peak in winter months.

Year-over-year revenue trend is volatile rather than strictly growing: slight decline in 2022, strong growth in 2023 and 2024, followed by a moderate decline in 2025.

ADR increased up to 2023 and then started to decrease, suggesting pricing or channel-mix changes in later periods.

Guest revenue ranking shows the top individual guests generate around 1k+ revenue each, but revenue is not extremely concentrated overall — about 201 guests account for roughly 80% of total revenue.

Monthly channel ranking varies over time: booking is dominant in most months, but direct bookings lead in some individual months, confirming channel performance shifts.

Window functions (RANK, LAG, running SUM, cumulative share) were successfully used to produce rankings, running totals, YoY change, and Pareto-style concentration metrics.
